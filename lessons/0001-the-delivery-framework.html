System Design · Lesson 01 · Phase 0 — Framework

The Delivery Framework

One repeatable spine for driving any 45-minute design interview — and where the L6/L7 bar actually sits.

Why this lesson, for your mission: Every one of the 27 case studies you'll work through hangs off this skeleton. Master the skeleton once and you never again walk into a design round wondering "what do I do first?" The framework is what lets you spend your energy on judgement instead of structure — which is exactly what staff interviewers grade.

The big idea

A design interview isn't a test of whether you know the "right" architecture. It's a test of whether you can navigate an open-ended problem under time pressure without getting lost. Hello Interview packages that navigation into six steps over roughly 35 minutes.[1] The clock matters: the single most common way candidates fail is running out of time because they added complexity too early and never reached a complete design.[1]

The six steps

1

Requirements ~5 min

Split into two lists. Functional: 3 core "users should be able to…" features, found by asking the interviewer targeted questions — FAANGs directly grade your ability to focus on what matters.[1] Non-functional: 3–5 system qualities (the "-ilities") — pull from a checklist: CAP priority, scale, latency, durability, consistency, fault tolerance, security.

Capacity estimation lives here too — but only do the math if it changes a design decision. Don't compute numbers for show.[1]

2

Core Entities ~2 min

Name the 2–3 primary nouns the system stores (User, Ride, Tweet…). Start small; you'll discover more as you design. Good names signal clear thinking.[1]

3

API / Interface ~5 min

Define the contract clients call. REST by default; GraphQL or RPC when justified. One endpoint per functional requirement, plural resource names: POST /v1/rides. Security rule: always derive the current user from the auth token, never from the request body.[1]

4

Data Flow ~5 min · optional

Only for data-processing systems (crawlers, pipelines, aggregators). List the sequential transform: Fetch → Parse → Extract → Store → Repeat. Skip it entirely for ordinary request/response apps.[1]

5

High-Level Design ~10–15 min

Boxes and arrows. Walk through your APIs one at a time, showing how each request flows through components and what state changes. Stay shallow on purpose — note hard spots out loud ("we'll need to handle hot partitions, I'll come back to that") and keep moving so you reach a complete system.[1] Only show schema columns that matter.

6

Deep Dives ~10 min

Now revisit the hard spots: meet the non-functional requirements, kill bottlenecks, handle edge cases. Scaling, caching, sharding, fan-out-on-read vs fan-out-on-write. Leave the interviewer room to probe — don't monologue.[1]

Where the L6/L7 bar sits

The framework is the same at every level — how you spend the minutes is not. Interviewers grade depth-vs-breadth differently as you go up:[2]

LevelBreadthDepthWhat it means
Mid (L4)80%20%A working HLD; components stay abstractions.
Senior (L5)60%40%Go deep where you have hands-on experience.
Staff+ (L6/L7)40%60%Most of the hour is deep dives, driven by you.
★ Staff-level signal For you, steps 1–5 are a fast on-ramp, not the main event. The four behaviors that separate staff from senior:[3]
  1. Simple by default. Reach for "is a sophisticated approach actually required?" before reaching for the complex design.
  2. Proactive depth. Expand into deep dives nobody asked for — you see the solution space and walk toward the hard parts unprompted.
  3. Judgement on alternatives. Name 2–3 options, pick one, say why, with the confidence of having shipped it.
  4. Don't explain the 101. If it's first-year-on-the-job knowledge, skip it unless asked. Spending time on basics reads as junior.

Tiny worked example: a paste bin

Watch the framework compress a whole design into a few lines:

1. FRCreate a paste, retrieve a paste by id, set expiry.
1. NFRRead-heavy, low latency on reads, highly available, pastes immutable.
2. EntitiesPaste, User.
3. APIPOST /v1/pastes · GET /v1/pastes/:id
5. HLDClient → API → blob store for content + metadata DB; CDN/cache in front of reads.
6. Deep diveHow to generate short unique ids? Cache strategy for hot pastes? Expiry sweep?

Notice: the deep-dive questions are where a staff candidate would spend most of the hour. We'll build the muscle to answer them over the coming case studies.

Check yourself — retrieval practice

Don't peek at the notes above. Pulling the answer from memory is what makes it stick.

1. What are the six steps of the delivery framework, in order?

The on-ramp narrows from "what / who" to "how": needs → nouns → contract → (flow) → boxes → depth.

2. At L6/L7, roughly how is your time split between breadth and depth?

Staff flips the mid-level ratio: most of the hour is deep dives, driven by you.

3. When should you do capacity-estimation math?

Math for show wastes minutes. Math that picks "one DB or ten" earns them.

4. Which behavior most signals staff-level, not senior?

Proactivity + simple-by-default is the signal. Over-explaining basics reads as junior.
