# System Design Resources

## Knowledge

- [Hello Interview — System Design in a Hurry](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/introduction)
  The spine of this whole curriculum. Use for: the delivery framework, core
  concepts, key technologies, common patterns, and all 27 case studies.
- [Hello Interview — Delivery Framework](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/in-a-hurry/delivery)
  The 6-step, ~35-min structure for driving any interview. Use for: the spine of
  every single design. Memorize this.
- [Hello Interview — What is Expected at Each Level](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/the-system-design-interview-what-is-expected-at-each-level)
  Breadth/depth ratios per level (mid 80/20, senior 60/40, staff 40/60). Use for:
  calibrating how deep to go and what signal to send for L6/L7.
- [Hello Interview — 5 Keys to Staff-Level System Design](https://www.hellointerview.com/blog/staff-level-system-design)
  What staff+ interviewers actually look for. Use for: proactivity, simple-by-
  default designs, judgement on alternatives, not over-explaining the 101.
- [Hello Interview — Problem Breakdowns (case studies)](https://www.hellointerview.com/learn/system-design/problem-breakdowns/bitly)
  The 27 worked case studies, grouped easy/medium/hard. Use for: the answer keys
  we'll work through one at a time.
- [Book: Designing Data-Intensive Applications — Martin Kleppmann](https://dataintensive.net/)
  The canonical deep-reference for the *why* behind storage, replication,
  partitioning, consistency, and stream processing. Use for: going beyond the
  interview answer into real understanding (matches the L6/L7 "I've built this" bar).

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/leetcode & r/ExperiencedDevs](https://reddit.com/r/ExperiencedDevs)
  Real interview debriefs and staff-level war stories. Use for: calibrating
  difficulty and reading recent FAANG interview reports.
- [Hello Interview — community / Discord](https://www.hellointerview.com/)
  Mock interview partners and feedback on recordings. Use for: testing your
  delivery out loud against another human (the only way to build real fluency).
